What If the World Went Solarpunk?

A city covered in glass leaves: every roof tilted to the sun, farms that grow food and power on the same field. The film asks whether the whole world could run on sunlight, and follows a solar panel from the price revolution through the night shift, winter, the factory and the funeral. This page lists every factual line in the film, with its source, and says plainly what is our own arithmetic, what is a model or a projection, and what nobody knows yet.

What the film claims, and where it comes from

In film order, one or two sentences per line, in the words of the narration. calc means we derived the number ourselves and re-ran the arithmetic. The glass leaves, the workforce that clocks in at sunrise and the night shift are metaphors; the morning in the solarpunk city is invented. Some sources were read directly, others through press coverage or search summaries because the publisher blocks automated readers; the notes say which. Chapter times follow the final cut.

0:00

A city covered in glass leaves

  1. Seven in the morning, and a whole city is switching on. Every roof is tilted toward the sun and covered in dark glass, and so is the farm on the roof next door.

    An invented scene, the solarpunk city the film imagines

  2. Like real leaves, they drink sunlight, but instead of sugar, they make electricity, with no smoke and no moving parts.

    Textbook physics (a silicon p-n junction); the leaf is a metaphor

  3. This is solarpunk, a future that looks less like a war movie and more like a greenhouse.

    Wikipedia, Solarpunk (it "offers counter-dystopian visions"; its look favours greens, blues and light). The war-movie line is ours

  4. Last year, the world put up about three million new solar panels. Every day.

    Ember, Global Electricity Review 2026, 21 April 2026 ("a new record for capacity additions of 647 GW in 2025")·647 GW ÷ 365 = 1.77 GW a day; at 600 W a panel that is 3.0 million, at 400 W it would be 4.4 million. The panel size is our assumption and is shown on screencalc

0:52

Leaves that never ask for a raise

  1. The dream got its name in an anonymous blog post, inspired by a cargo ship pulled across the sea partly by a giant kite.

    Wikipedia, Solarpunk ("An anonymous author coined the term in the blog post 'From Steampunk to Solarpunk' (April 30, 2008), taking inspiration from the MS Beluga Skysails kite-powered ship")

  2. A rebellion against a rebellion, in a critic's words, after decades of neon-and-smog cyberpunk.

    James Machell, Encyclopedia of Science Fiction, 2024 ("a rebellion against a rebellion, born out of dystopia fatigue"), as quoted by Wikipedia. One critic's reading

  3. Inside, light knocks electrons loose in a thin slice of silicon, and the slice lets them out through a single door, into a wire: the photovoltaic effect.

    Textbook physics, simplified: the "single door" is the p-n junction. The first practical silicon cell, shown on screen, was made at Bell Labs in 1954: IEEE Milestone, First Practical Photovoltaic Solar Cell

  4. The best rooftop leaves turn just under a quarter of their light into power.

    Fraunhofer ISE, Photovoltaics Report, 14 July 2026 (commercial monocrystalline modules rose "from about 17% to just under 25%")

  5. Stack a thin crystal called perovskite on top, a second worker on the same shift, and lab cells pass a third.

    Fraunhofer ISE, Photovoltaics Report (record lab cell "for Pero-Si 35.2%"). A lab record, not a product; it passes the 33.7% limit for a single-layer cell because it stacks two

  6. In the mid-seventies, the cells for a single modern rooftop panel's worth of power would have cost about fifty thousand of today's dollars.

    Our World in Data, solar PV module prices (1975: $132.38 per watt in constant 2025 dollars; from Nemet 2009, Farmer & Lafond 2016, IRENA 2025)·$132.38 × 400 W = $52,952calc

  7. Today, they cost about a hundred.

    Our World in Data (2024: $0.27 per watt)·$0.265 × 400 W = $106; a 99.8% dropcalc·Module only, not an installed system

  1. And every time the world builds twice as many, the price drops by about a fifth: a machine that gets cheaper the more of it we build.

    The solar learning rate: about 20% per doubling of installed capacity in Our World in Data's analysis, 24.9% for 1976 to 2023 in the industry roadmap ITRPV, both from our research notes. "About a fifth" is the low end

  2. Last year, glass leaves made almost nine percent of the world's electricity.

    Ember, Global Electricity Review 2026 (solar rose "from 2,143 TWh in 2024 to 2,778 TWh in 2025")·Fraunhofer ISE (8.7% of world gross electricity in 2025)

  3. In a single year, Pakistan imported about seventeen gigawatts of panels, twice the year before, as households fled the grid's prices.

    Ember, Global Electricity Review 2025, 8 April 2025 ("The country imported 17 GW of solar panels in 2024 to meet this growing consumer demand, double the amount imported the year before"). Ember ties the boom to high grid tariffs; imports are not installations, see the notes

  4. No manifesto, no green roofs, just cheaper bills.

    The film’s gloss on the Pakistan boom above: a price-driven rooftop rush, not an ideology

2:43

The night shift

  1. More and more, the night shift goes to batteries, shipping containers that fill up at noon and empty after dark.

    IEA, Global Energy Review 2026: Battery storage ("In 2025, 108 GW of new battery storage capacity was deployed worldwide, 40% more than in 2024"). Batteries cover hours to a day or two, not a winter

  2. In sunny places, the night shift is starting to look doable, at least on paper. A model for Las Vegas over-hired panels, added a field of batteries, and kept the power steady for ninety-seven percent of the hours in a year.

    Ember, Solar electricity every hour of every day is here, 21 June 2025 ("Las Vegas can reach 97% of the way to 1 GW constant supply … using 6 GW solar panels and 17 GWh battery", at about $104/MWh). A model, hence "at least on paper"

  3. Far from the equator, the glass leaves go part-time for months, and sometimes the wind drops at the same time.

    Kittel, Roth & Schill, "Long-duration electricity storage needs for coping with Dunkelflaute events in Europe", Nature Communications, 9 May 2026 (the study is about Dunkelflaute, the dark, windless spells of a European winter)

  4. To survive the worst dark, windless winter of recent decades, a Europe running mostly on wind and sun, one study says, would need to store three to seven percent of a whole year's electricity.

    Kittel, Roth & Schill, "Long-duration electricity storage needs for coping with Dunkelflaute events in Europe", Nature Communications, 9 May 2026 ("351 terawatt hours long-duration storage capacity, corresponding to 7% of yearly European electricity demand"; "159 TWh … around 3% of yearly electric load" with unlimited balancing; worst case the winter of 1996/97, as summarised in our research notes)

  5. Weeks of power for an entire continent, saved up like grain in a barn.

    3% of 365 days is 11 days; 7% is 26 dayscalc

  1. What would hold it is still an open question. Hydrogen, giant reservoirs, longer power lines, nuclear: every candidate has fans, and none has yet done the job at that scale.

    Our research summary: storage studies disagree on the size and the technology (another study, the Royal Society’s, puts UK hydrogen storage at 60 to 100 TWh). Pumped hydro and the rest exist; none stores weeks for a continent today

  2. In Germany, the day shift reaches all the way down to panels clipped onto balcony railings.

    Fraunhofer ISE, Photovoltaics Report (about 5.7 million PV systems in Germany, balcony systems included; PV 16.9% of net electricity in 2025)

  3. In one German trial, panels went up on stilts above a field, and the crops underneath got nearly a third less light.

    Fraunhofer ISE, Agrophotovoltaics: High Harvesting Yield in Hot Summer of 2018, 12 April 2019 ("The solar irradiation underneath the APV system was about 30 percent less than the reference field"), Heggelbach near Lake Constance

  4. Even so, growing the same food and power on separate fields would have needed sixty to eighty-six percent more land.

    Fraunhofer ISE, Agrophotovoltaics: High Harvesting Yield in Hot Summer of 2018, 12 April 2019 ("The results from 2017 showed a land use efficiency of 160 percent", rising "to 186 percent" in 2018). 160% to 186% means 60% to 86% more land if grown apart

  1. By one rough estimate, an electrified world would need a square of desert panels about seven hundred and thirty kilometres on each side.

    250 W/m² of average desert sun × 22% efficiency × 40% ground cover × 0.8 performance = 17.6 W per m² of land; half of today’s 592 EJ a year is 9.4 TW; 9.4 TW ÷ 17.6 W/m² = 533,000 km², a square 730 km a sidecalc·The 592 EJ is the Energy Institute’s 2024 world primary energy, from our research notes; "half" is our assumption for an electrified world (electric cars and heat pumps waste less). All of today’s energy would need a square 1,030 km a side

  2. Roughly the size of Spain, give or take.

    533,000 km² against Spain’s roughly 506,000 km²calc

  3. Spread over roofs, car parks and fields instead, it's about a third of a percent of the world's land.

    533,000 km² ÷ 149 million km² of land = 0.36%calc

  1. Still, ground panels need far more land per unit of energy than a nuclear plant.

    Our World in Data, How does the land use of different electricity sources compare?, 2022 (nuclear "needs … 18 to 27-times less than on-ground solar PV" per unit of electricity). Rooftop panels use no extra land

  2. And some places have already refused solar farms on good farmland.

    Farm and Dairy, 20 December 2022 (the Ohio Power Siting Board denied a 175 MW solar farm "would have covered 1,500 acres" in Greene County). The board cited local opposition; "prime farmland" is from our research notes, not this article

5:12

The life and death of a glass leaf

  1. A glass leaf starts as sand, cooked in electric furnaces into ultra-pure silicon, polysilicon.

    IEA, Solar PV Global Supply Chains, 2022 ("electricity accounts for over 40% of production costs for polysilicon")

  2. Most of the factory power comes from coal, and China runs more than four fifths of every step.

    IEA, Solar PV Global Supply Chains ("coal generates over 60% of the electricity used for global solar PV manufacturing"; China's share "in all the manufacturing stages" exceeds 80%)

  1. A few years ago, about forty percent of the world's polysilicon came from a single far-western region.

    IEA, Solar PV Global Supply Chains, 2022 ("Xinjiang accounts for 40% global polysilicon manufacturing")

  2. There, researchers linked some factories to state-run transfers of minority workers.

    Murphy & Elimä, In Broad Daylight: Uyghur Forced Labour and Global Solar Supply Chains, Sheffield Hallam University, May 2021 (polysilicon makers in the region used or sourced from state-sponsored labour transfers; about 45% of world solar-grade polysilicon). A university report, not peer-reviewed

  3. China denies it, and the researchers say the forced-labour risk hasn't gone.

    Crawford & Murphy, Over-Exposed, Sheffield Hallam University, November 2023 (the region's share fell to about 35% in 2022 but "represents significant exposure to state-imposed forced labor for the industry"). China's denial is reported in our research notes

  4. Still, a glass leaf repays the energy it took to make in about a year or so.

    Fraunhofer ISE, Photovoltaics Report (energy payback about 1.1 years in northern Europe, longer for Chinese-made modules; over 20 years a system makes about twenty times the energy invested)

  5. It slows a little every year, and after twenty-five years or more, it retires, tired but still working.

    NREL fleet study (about 2,500 US sites): median loss 0.75% a year; Jordan et al., 2011: 0.5% a year; from our research notes·Fraunhofer ISE (assumes 25 years and 0.7% a year)·0.75% a year for 25 years leaves about 83% of the original outputcalc

  6. A retired leaf is mostly glass and aluminium, with a sliver of silicon and traces of copper and silver.

    Fraunhofer ISE (by mass: glass 67.45%, aluminium frame 12.73%, silicon 2.73%, copper 1.3%, silver about 0.03%; plastics make up most of the rest)

  7. The toxic bits, lead or sometimes cadmium, are under a tenth of a percent of its weight.

    Mirletz et al., "Unfounded concerns about photovoltaic module toxicity and waste are slowing decarbonization", Nature Physics 19, 2023 (toxic materials are under 0.1% of module mass; paywalled, figure as reported in coverage of the paper)·Fraunhofer ISE (lead 0.09%). Cadmium is in cadmium-telluride panels, a minority technology

  8. By mid-century, retired leaves could add up to somewhere between fifty and a hundred and sixty million tonnes, depending on how long panels last.

    Mirletz et al., "Unfounded concerns about photovoltaic module toxicity and waste are slowing decarbonization", Nature Physics 19, 2023 (54 to 160 million tonnes by 2050, depending on module lifetime, as reported by NREL; paywalled)

  9. A well-known estimate of seventy-eight million works out at about ten kilos for every person alive.

    IRENA and IEA-PVPS, End-of-Life Management: Solar Photovoltaic Panels, 2016 (78 million tonnes by 2050)·78 billion kg ÷ 8.2 billion people = 9.5 kgcalc

  10. A real mountain, though a molehill next to coal ash.

    Mirletz et al., "Unfounded concerns about photovoltaic module toxicity and waste are slowing decarbonization", Nature Physics 19, 2023 (coal ash is 300 to 800 times larger by mass, as quoted in coverage; paywalled)

  1. And almost all of it could be recycled. Today, most recyclers keep little more than the glass and frame, and the cells mostly become waste.

    Fraunhofer ISE ("It is technically feasible to recycle and reuse almost 100% of the materials… For economic reasons, however, only the glass, frame and junction box (J-Box) are recycled today")

  2. At recent prices, a panel's few grams of silver are worth more than the glass, frame and silicon combined.

    pv magazine, 11 June 2026, quoting Obst of Fraunhofer CSP ("Silver accounts for just 0.03% of a panel's mass" but is worth "approximately EUR 600 per module", against "around EUR 34" of glass). See the notes on the unit·0.03% of 11.6 kg is about 3.5 g of silver per square metre of panelcalc

  3. Getting it out can still cost more than dumping the panel.

    Heath et al., "Research and development priorities for silicon photovoltaic module recycling to support a circular economy", Nature Energy, 2020 (recycling must cost less than disposal to take off; from our research notes). True where landfill is cheap, not where the law requires recycling, hence "can"

  4. Worse, nobody even knows how many panels get recycled worldwide.

    Our research found estimates from under 10% (Yale Environment 360) to about 14% (an IEA figure relayed by the trade press) and no consistent global statistic

  5. In 2024, German plants with room for more than fifty thousand tonnes got fewer than ten thousand.

    pv magazine, 1 October 2026, on a Fraunhofer CSP / DERA study ("more than 50,000 tonnes of recycling capacity"; "around 9,300 tonnes of decommissioned modules underwent initial processing" in 2024)·9,300 ÷ 50,000 = 19% of capacitycalc

  6. Some may have been exported, and where used panels do arrive, as in parts of Africa, there is almost nothing to recycle them with.

    pv magazine, 1 October 2026, on a Fraunhofer CSP / DERA study (the shortfall may be explained by "the export of used modules"; not measured)·For Africa: a UCL paper found no PV recycling plants in sub-Saharan Africa; South Africa has e-waste firms that dismantle panels (from our research notes)

  7. And the night shift's batteries will have to retire somewhere too.

    A statement of the obvious: our research found no reliable recycling rate for grid batteries, so the film makes no number claim

7:28

Towns that went a little punk

  1. Some new recycling lines report recovering over nine tenths of the silicon and silver.

    pv magazine USA, 6 May 2026, on an IEA-PVPS Task 12 report ("SPR reports recovery of 98 weight percent … of input silicon"; "Solarcycle reports recovery of nearly 92 wt. % for silver"; First Solar over 90% of its semiconductor). Company-reported

  2. Designers are working on swapping out the lead and the silver, so the next generation could die cleaner.

    Fraunhofer ISE (lead-free solder, copper in place of silver, antimony-free glass, fluorine-free backsheets, frameless modules). Research and development, not yet the norm

  3. And in Europe, the law already wants at least four fifths of every dead panel recycled.

    EU WEEE Directive 2012/19/EU (Annex V: for large equipment, the category panels fall in, "80 % shall be prepared for re-use and recycled")·Fraunhofer ISE ("The European WEEE Directive stipulates at least 80%"). Glass alone is most of that weight

  4. Some towns aren't waiting: in a Freiburg neighbourhood, people own far fewer cars.

    Christian Science Monitor, 20 December 2006 (Vauban "only 150 per 1,000 inhabitants", against 430 in Freiburg)·City of Freiburg, Vauban information boards (172 cars per 1,000 residents, 2013 statistics)

  5. And next door, solar homes make more energy over a year than they use.

    Rolf Disch, The Solar Settlement ("a complete housing estate of Plusenergy Houses")·Wikipedia, Solar Settlement at Schlierberg ("all the homes produce a positive energy balance"). Over a year, not every hour

  1. In Abu Dhabi, a zero-carbon desert city was still mostly unbuilt a decade later, and its builders dropped the zero-carbon goal, a showcase planned from the top down.

    The Guardian, 16 February 2016 ("less than 5% of the original six square km" built; developers "have abandoned their original goal of building the world's first zero-carbon city"; completion "pushed back to 2030"). "Top down" is the film's reading

  2. One of solarpunk's early writers calls that fake solarpunk urbanism: green roofs on luxury flats that push locals out.

    Wikipedia, Solarpunk (Adam Flynn, citing Vice 2021 and Resilience 2022: luxury green-roof condos that displace communities as "fake solarpunk urbanism")

  3. The sunlight is the easy part. Every couple of hours, the Sun gives our planet as much energy as humanity uses in a year.

    Humanity used 592 EJ in 2024, which is 164,444 TWh; sunlight at the top of the atmosphere is about 173,000 TW (under an hour), and at the surface about 89,000 TW (1.85 hours)calc·Energy Institute, Statistical Review of World Energy 2025 (total energy demand "reaching a new high of 592 EJ"). The 173,000 TW figure is a common textbook value

  4. A world powered mostly by the sun looks possible, and is partly here already. Sunny days and short nights, it is learning to handle, but long winters and dead leaves, not yet.

    The film’s conclusion, drawn from the claims above: the Las Vegas model (17), the winter study (19) and the recycling figures (42 to 48). Hedged with “looks possible”

  5. A real forest doesn't just grow leaves, it takes them back and turns them into soil for the next ones. Ours mostly don't, yet.

    Basic ecology, used as a metaphor for recycling

  6. The axolotl's balcony leaf will retire around the time a child born today finishes university.

    A 25 to 30 year panel life against about 22 years to finish a first degree; approximatecalc

  7. Whether it becomes the next leaf or landfill is being decided now, in recycling plants and in laws. Solarpunk isn't a city covered in glass leaves. It's a city that knows what to do when they fall.

    The channel’s view, not a claim

Simplifications and honest caveats

Nine and a half minutes is short. Here is what we left out, simplified, calculated ourselves or could not confirm.

  • Uncertain

    "Three million panels a day" depends on the panel

    Ember counts gigawatts, not panels: 647 GW added in 2025, about 1.77 GW a day. At 600 W per panel (a modern utility module) that is about 3 million a day; at 400 W (a rooftop module) about 4.4 million. The film uses the lower figure and shows the assumption on screen.

  • Simplified

    The price is the module, not the system

    The $50,000 and $100 are module prices for 400 W of panels, in constant 2025 dollars, from Our World in Data. They leave out inverters, mounting, labour and permits, which now cost more than the panels. Part of the latest fall (module prices down more than 60% since 2023, per IEA-PVPS) is Chinese overcapacity, not only learning.

  • Simplified

    Nine percent of electricity is not nine percent of energy

    Solar made about 8.7% of the world's electricity in 2025, and electricity is only about a fifth of the energy the world uses. Solar's share of all energy is much smaller.

  • Uncertain

    Pakistan: imports are not installations

    About 17 GW of panels were imported in 2024 (another report says 16 GW). Official installed capacity is far lower, and nobody knows exactly how many imported panels went up on roofs. We cut an analyst's claim that the imports equalled half the country's peak demand.

  • Uncertain

    Las Vegas is a model, not a power plant

    Ember modelled a stand-alone plant with 2024 prices: 6 GW of panels and 17 GWh of batteries for a steady 1 GW, 97% of hours on average (95 to 99% depending on the year). That is about six times more panels than the output, which critics point out. Nobody has built it.

  • Contested

    How much winter storage, and in what

    The 3 to 7% (159 to 351 TWh) is one study's answer for one worst case, the winter of 1996/97, in a Europe running mostly on wind and sun. Other studies give lower figures. Which technology would store it, and how often such winters come, are open questions; the film says "one study says".

  • Uncertain

    The 730 km square is our own estimate

    We assumed 250 W/m² of average desert sun, 22% efficient panels, 40% ground cover, 80% performance and that an electrified world needs half of today's primary energy. Change the assumptions and the side ranges from about 730 to 1,030 km. Nobody proposes one square: transmission, storage and politics make it a picture, not a plan. That is why the film cut "the famous square in the Sahara".

  • Simplified

    Agrivoltaics: one small trial

    Heggelbach is a single trial on a third of a hectare. The 60 to 86% extra land comes from two years (2017 and the hot, dry 2018). In 2017 the potato, wheat and celeriac yields under the panels were 18 to 19% lower, which the film does not say. One critic notes a hectare of agri-PV gives about 82% of the electricity of an ordinary solar park.

  • Contested

    Forced-labour risk in Xinjiang

    The 40% share of polysilicon is the IEA's 2022 figure; Sheffield Hallam's later report says the region's share of world solar-grade polysilicon fell from about 45% (2020) to 35% (2022). The labour-transfer findings come from university researchers, not a peer-reviewed paper, and China denies them. Audits in the region are restricted. The film says "linked" and gives both sides.

  • Uncertain

    The waste mountain is a projection

    How much panel waste there will be depends mostly on how long panels last: older forecasts assumed 12-year lifetimes, newer ones 30 to 35 years or more. The range in the film (54 to 160 million tonnes by 2050) is from one peer-reviewed paper by NREL staff, written as a rebuttal; IRENA's 2016 figure is 78 million. A newer figure of over 200 million tonnes, reported in the trade press for an IRENA 1.5°C scenario, could not be checked against IRENA and was not used.

  • Uncertain

    The silver surprise depends on the price

    The comparison (silver about €600 per module against €34 of glass) is a Fraunhofer researcher's, at a silver price of about $68 an ounce. Those euro figures do not match 3.5 g of silver per square metre, so the unit is probably not one ordinary module; we could not resolve it. The film keeps the qualitative point and says "at recent prices".

  • Uncertain

    Recycling rates and exports nobody has measured

    There is no reliable global figure for how many panels are recycled. Germany's shortfall (about 9,300 t processed against more than 50,000 t of capacity, 19%) may be explained by exports of used panels, but nobody has measured that. We found no study showing how many exported panels become waste in Africa, so the film does not say "dumped"; it says there is almost nothing to recycle them with.

  • Simplified

    Recycling records are company-reported

    The over-90% recoveries (SPR 98% silicon, Solarcycle about 92% silver, First Solar over 90% of its semiconductor) are reported by the companies and gathered by IEA-PVPS, mostly at pilot or early commercial scale. The EU's 80% target is by weight, and glass alone gets close to it.

  • Simplified

    Real places are messier

    Vauban's car figures (about 150 to 220 cars per 1,000 residents against 430 to 520 in Freiburg) come from reports of 2006 to 2009 and vary by source; it is not car-free, and rents rose. The Solar Settlement's surplus is over a year, not every hour. Reports of how much of Masdar was built differ (under 5% of 6 km² is one); it does have a working research campus.

  • Simplified

    "A couple of hours" of sunlight

    The popular line says one hour of sunlight equals a year of human energy use. That holds at the top of the atmosphere; about a third is reflected and more is absorbed on the way down, so at the surface it is closer to two hours. The film says "a couple of hours".

  • Simplified

    Source access

    We read the Fraunhofer ISE report, the IEA supply-chain summary, the Our World in Data prices, Ember's 2026 review and the Wikipedia article directly. Many other figures (Ember's 24/7 model and its Pakistan numbers, the winter-storage study, Heggelbach, the NREL waste paper, IRENA 2016, the Sheffield Hallam reports, the pv magazine pieces, Masdar and Vauban) were first read through press coverage or search summaries; on 28 October 2026 we found and opened the original pages linked here, except where a publisher blocks automated readers (the Nature Physics paper is paywalled, so its figures are as reported by NREL and the press; EUR-Lex and the Energy Institute were confirmed through other copies of the same text). The 1996/97 winter and the China denial come from our research notes.

  • Left out

    Stylisation, and what we left out

    The glass leaves, the workforce, the night shift and the funeral are metaphors; the city and its axolotl are invented. Left out: Samsø and Wildpoldsried (mostly wind and biomass), Singapore's rooftop targets, the Moss Landing battery fire, the debate over silver reserves, whether perovskite tandems last 25 years outdoors, and land disputes we could not source.
